Optical Correction Simulation

Simulation Specifications & Theoretical Assumptions
  • Eye Approximation: Collapses the cornea, aqueous humor, crystalline lens, and vitreous humor into a single refracting surface with a uniform refractive index (n = 1.336).
  • Paraxial Approximation: Assumes incoming light rays are close to the optical axis and travel at shallow angles, allowing standard thin-lens vergence equations to hold true.
  • Principal Plane Rendering: By convention, refraction is visually calculated at flat vertical planes (principal planes) rather than calculating exact curved surface intersection physics via Snell's Law.

Interactive Explorations

Try these exercises to test your understanding of optical principles:

1. The High-Minus Dilemma:
Set the Corrective Lens Power to -10.00 D. Watch the Retinal Focal Shift values as you move the Vertex Distance slider back and forth from 10mm to 20mm. Now try the same thing with a -2.00 D lens. Why does the focus move so much more drastically with the higher prescription?
2. Correcting Myopia:
Reset the lens to 0.00 D. If you increase the eye's Axial Length to 24mm, what mathematical sign (+ or -) must the corrective lens have to push the focal point back onto the retina? Try to guess the answer, test it out with the slider, and verify using the Auto-Refract tool.
