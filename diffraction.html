Visual Theory & Clinical Context

> AIRY'S DISK VS. NEWTON'S RINGS The circular aperture projection looks almost identical to Newton's Rings, but the physics are completely different:

• Airy's Disk (Diffraction): Created by a flat wave of light squeezing through a hole. The central spot contains ~84% of the light, and the outer rings fade exponentially into darkness.
• Newton's Rings (Thin-Film Interference): Created by light reflecting off a microscopic air gap between a curved lens and flat glass. The outer rings do not fade exponentially; they maintain brightness but squeeze closer together.
> EXE: White Light Dispersion Toggle White Light mode. Notice how the central maximum remains pure white, but the outer fringes become spectral rainbows? This happens because violet light (λ ≈ 400 nm) reaches its destructive interference minimum at a smaller angle than red light (λ ≈ 700 nm).
> EXE: The Aberration Trade-off Switch to Circular (Pupil). The human pupil ranges from 2 mm to 8 mm. A tiny pupil causes massive diffraction blur (Airy Disks). But a massive pupil exposes the outer edges of the cornea, causing Spherical Aberrations. The eye reaches peak resolution at a pupil diameter of ~3.0 mm, perfectly balancing both physical constraints.
