Visual Theory & Clinical Context

> AIRY'S DISK VS. NEWTON'S RINGS The circular aperture projection looks almost identical to Newton's Rings, but the physics are completely different:

• Airy pattern (diffraction): Produced by Fraunhofer diffraction at an ideal circular aperture. About 84% of the transmitted energy lies inside the central disc up to its first dark ring. The outer rings become progressively weaker according to the squared Bessel-function intensity distribution; the decrease is not exponential.
• Newton's rings (thin-film interference): Produced by interference between light reflected from the two surfaces of a thin air film between a plano-convex lens and a flat plate. In this ideal reflected-light model, the centre is dark and the rings become closer together outward. This is a separate physical model from the Airy pattern.
> EXE: White-Light Diffraction Toggle White Light mode. The central maximum remains approximately white because the sampled wavelengths overlap at the centre. Away from the centre, the minima occur at different angles: shorter wavelengths (violet) reach a given diffraction minimum closer to the centre than longer wavelengths (red). The displayed spectrum is an illustrative seven-wavelength RGB approximation, not a calibrated white-light spectrum or a prism-dispersion model.
> EXE: Pupil Diameter and Resolution Switch to Circular Aperture (Pupil). The control now represents pupil diameter in millimetres (2–8 mm). A smaller pupil increases diffraction blur; a larger pupil reduces diffraction but may reveal more optical aberrations in a real eye. The balance varies with the individual eye, accommodation, illumination and other conditions, so there is no single universal pupil diameter that guarantees peak resolution.
> MODEL ASSUMPTIONS The slit and circular-aperture patterns use ideal Fraunhofer diffraction with a fixed 1.00 m screen distance. In circular mode, the graph and 2D pattern keep a fixed +/-1.5 mrad display field (about +/-1.5 mm on the screen), so changes in pupil diameter remain visible at the same scale. The displayed width formulas are paraxial estimates. White-light mode combines seven sampled wavelengths with a simplified RGB mapping. The Newton's-rings reference is a separate ideal reflected-light model for an air film, with a fixed 1.0 m lens radius of curvature and a displayed radial range of 0–2 mm; it is independent of pupil diameter. These are teaching models, not calibrated laboratory predictions.
