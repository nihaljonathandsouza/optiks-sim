Principle of Superposition

y(t) = y1(t) + y2(t)

For two identical waves with a phase difference φ:

y1 = A·sin(ωt)
y2 = A·sin(ωt + φ)

At φ = 180° (π radians):

A·sin(ωt) + A·sin(ωt + π) = 0

Timbre & Harmonics (Fourier Theory)

> Why do shapes sound different?

French mathematician Joseph Fourier proved that every complex wave is just a sum of pure sine waves. Changing the waveform shape changes the Harmonics (overtones) layered on top of the base frequency:

  • Sine: Only the fundamental frequency. Clean and smooth.
  • Triangle: Odd harmonics that drop rapidly. Warm.
  • Square: Odd harmonics dropping slowly. Reedy "8-bit" sound.
  • Sawtooth: ALL harmonics. Extremely bright and buzzy.
> The DJ Bass Wobble

Click the DJ WOBBLE button. This switches the DSP to a rich Sawtooth wave at a low 55 Hz sub-bass, and offsets the anti-wave by +3.0 Hz. Because the waves oscillate at different speeds, they rapidly drift in and out of phase, creating the classic throbbing "wub-wub" acoustic beat heavily utilized in electronic music.

Hardware Limitations

> Real-World Music ANC

Load the ADAMAN track or upload a file and click Play. Check the Phase Inversion box to simulate the ANC micro-processor mathematically inverting the wave. The music will go perfectly silent, and the Resultant oscilloscope will flatline.

> The DSP Latency Problem

Slide the Hardware Latency up slightly. Notice how the sound bleeds back in? The digital processor needs microseconds to calculate the inversion. For high frequencies (short wavelengths), a tiny delay means the anti-noise arrives too late, accidentally causing constructive interference. This is why ANC struggles with sudden, high-pitched noises but easily blocks low-frequency engine hums.

Live Environmental Testing

> Testing Real-World ANC

With headphones on, start the microphone. Snap your fingers or speak into the microphone to see the raw wave on the top oscilloscope. When you check Phase Inversion, the system will attempt to actively cancel the ambient noise entering your microphone before it reaches your headphones. Because your laptop processes this via Web Audio API, the inherent latency of your operating system (usually 10-30ms) will act as an unavoidable "Hardware Delay," making perfect cancellation of high-frequency speech impossible, perfectly mirroring the physics problem faced by engineers building real ANC headsets.
